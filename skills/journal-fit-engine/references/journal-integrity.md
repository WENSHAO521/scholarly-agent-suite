# Journal Integrity & Discontinuation Status

## Publication-integrity screening

Every recommendation should include an integrity check, not just a fit check.
Signals to consider:

- publisher identity (real, traceable organization vs. opaque/unverifiable)
- editorial transparency (named editorial board, verifiable affiliations)
- peer-review information (described process, plausible timelines)
- archiving (participation in a recognized digital-preservation scheme)
- ISSN validity (registered, matches the journal's actual title/publisher)
- indexing claims (verify against the index's own listing, don't take the
  journal's word for it — see [indexing-metrics.md](indexing-metrics.md))
- contact transparency (real address, verifiable editorial contact)
- retraction/correction policy (exists and is followed)
- publication-ethics statement (exists, references a recognized standard,
  e.g. COPE)

## Labeling uncertainty honestly

Do not label a journal "predatory" without adequate, specific evidence — that
is a strong claim with reputational consequences and false positives are
harmful. When evidence is incomplete or ambiguous, use one of:

- `LOW INTEGRITY CONFIDENCE`
- `NEEDS VERIFICATION`
- `SIGNIFICANT TRANSPARENCY CONCERNS`

State exactly which signals are missing or concerning rather than issuing a
blanket verdict.

## Discontinuation and status detection

Where current evidence exists, detect and surface:

- `ceased` — journal no longer publishes
- `discontinued` — formally ended by publisher
- `renamed` — continues under a new title (link the successor)
- `merged` — combined with another journal (identify the resulting venue)
- `inactive` — no recent output, unclear ongoing status

Do not recommend an inactive, ceased, discontinued, or unclear-status venue
as a current submission target. If a formerly-relevant journal shows up in
reference-neighborhood analysis but is no longer active, say so and route to
its successor if one exists, or drop it from the candidate pool.

## Journal identity confusion — integrity angle

Because predatory operations sometimes deliberately mimic a legitimate
journal's name, treat title-only matches with extra caution during integrity
screening. Prefer ISSN-based identity resolution (see
[journal-profile.md](journal-profile.md)) specifically to avoid recommending
a look-alike journal by mistake.
