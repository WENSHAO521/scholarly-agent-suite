# Adaptation Policy

## Adaptation types

Journal-targeted adaptations may touch: title, abstract, introduction,
contribution framing, theory presentation, methods transparency, word count,
discussion, policy implications, clinical implications, limitations,
reference style, and section structure.

**Never recommend changing the scientific findings, data, results, sample, or
legal/historical evidence to fit a journal.** Adaptation is about framing and
presentation, not about the underlying scholarly content or truth.

## Word-count, article-type, abstract, and reference compatibility

- **Word count** — record current manuscript word count, the journal's stated
  limit, and what the journal excludes from the count (references, tables,
  abstract, etc.) if specified. If the journal's counting rule is ambiguous,
  say so rather than inventing an assumed rule.
- **Article type** — check the journal's *actual* category names (Original
  Article, Research Article, Review, Perspective, Brief Report, Research
  Note, Methods, Commentary, etc.) — do not assume these are interchangeable
  across journals; "Review" means different things at different venues.
- **Abstract** — check structured vs. unstructured format, word limit,
  required headings, whether a graphical abstract is required, and keyword
  requirements, from official instructions.
- **Reference style** — do not reject a candidate journal merely because its
  citation style differs from the manuscript's current style. Distinguish
  **structural mismatch** (e.g. word count, article type — genuinely hard to
  fix) from **formatting mismatch** (citation style, section headers —
  low-cost to adapt) and weight them very differently in the fit verdict.

## Adaptation cost

Classify overall required adaptation as `LOW`, `MODERATE`, or `HIGH` per
candidate, based on the scope of changes needed — this feeds directly into
submission-ladder sequencing (see [submission-strategy.md](submission-strategy.md)).

## Submission readiness

Track separately from fit: `READY`, `MINOR_ADAPTATION`, `MAJOR_ADAPTATION`,
`NOT_READY`.

## Ethical boundaries

Do not advise, suggest, or normalize:

- citation padding (adding citations to a target journal purely to please
  editors, not because they're substantively relevant)
- coercive citation compliance (adding citations because an editor demanded
  them without merit)
- fake international co-authors
- data manipulation of any kind
- duplicate or simultaneous submission where the journal prohibits it
- salami slicing (splitting one study into minimum-publishable-units purely
  to inflate publication count)
- misrepresenting methods actually used

If a user asks for help with any of these, decline and explain why, and offer
the legitimate alternative (e.g. a sequential submission ladder instead of
simultaneous submission).

## Medicine-specific reporting compliance

Where genuinely applicable to the manuscript's study design, consider
relevant reporting guidelines: CONSORT (RCTs), STROBE (observational),
PRISMA (systematic reviews/meta-analyses), CARE (case reports), TRIPOD
(prediction models), STARD (diagnostic accuracy), ARRIVE (animal research).
Do not force an irrelevant guideline onto a study design it wasn't designed
for.

## AI/CS-specific venue types

For AI/ML and CS more broadly, distinguish journal, conference, workshop, and
transactions venues — some subfields treat top conferences as the primary
venue, not a secondary one. This Skill is journal-first in its current
version but should note when a conference-first strategy is actually more
appropriate for the manuscript's subfield, rather than forcing a journal-only
recommendation onto a field where that's not how the community publishes.
